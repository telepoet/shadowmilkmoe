Long story: Shadow Milk Cookie's longing to connect with the Witches again permeates everything he does. From his time as an emissary to his corruption into a Beast, the disappearance of his creators is at the forefront of his mind and the core influence of his decision-making. His research as the Fount is reflective of his longing to understand them; his destructive tendencies as Shadow Milk conversely is lashing out at their silence, hoping that by being outlandish and cruel enough to the world they had cultivated, they would eventually have no choice but to come back.

Come being sealed in the Silver Tree, Shadow Milk understood his punishment to be their ONLY response after hundreds of years of nothing, which soured his opinion to the point of expressing a desire to see out their collective deaths - but I believe this to be a front, given Shadow Milk lies just as much to himself as he does to others, so to swallow his crushing abandonment down with a false bravado is definitely not off the table; and his current goals in Timeline of Fate actually readily reflect this.

Between the near-death experience and the revelation that the punishment Shadow Milk believed the Witches had enacted was instead at the hands of another cookie, he was already in a rather severe state of mind - his despair-induced and self-destructive patterns, his feelings of confusion and betrayal, now amplified to the worst possible extreme by The Ultimate Dough (and doubtless further influenced by the three extra Soul Jams now in his possession).

The most interesting thing to examine with this in mind is specifically how the Ultimate Dough seeks out one's "purest desires." Isolated out of his insanity-induced ramblings, with each new snippet of info we get about Shadow Milk's grand plan to become "the God and Messiah of Earthbread", the more it starts to sound more like a last-ditch effort to find the Witches. Warped by the Ultimate Dough, his hope is that by following the steps of these creation mythos - something that the Fount was likely taught by the WitchesReligious trauma being a prevalent theme of his character is more or less a no-brainer at this point, but the fact he's had a negative experience as both the worshipper AND worshipped is really something else. - that this extreme of a call to action would finally FORCE the Witches to come back, because that is what they are destined to do, textually. That is how their role in his script MUST play out, as the creators described within.
